Series #14
Thursday, November 12 • 16:15 – 17:15 Math Building, Room 204 & Hybrid on Zoom

An Intuitive Introduction to Ricci Curvature and Bochner's Formula on Compact Manifolds $(\mathcal{M}, g)$

Speaker: Elena Rossi (2nd-year Master's Student)

Curvature in Riemannian geometry is often introduced through the Riemann curvature tensor $R(X, Y)Z$, which carries 20 independent components in dimension 4. In this talk, we focus on the Ricci tensor $\operatorname{Ric}(X, Y) = \operatorname{tr}(Z \mapsto R(Z, X)Y)$, an average measure of sectional curvature that directly controls volume growth.

We will develop the classical Bochner-Weitzenböck identity for harmonic 1-forms $\omega \in \Omega^1(\mathcal{M})$:

$$\frac{1}{2}\Delta |\omega|^2 = |\nabla \omega|^2 + \operatorname{Ric}(\omega^\sharp, \omega^\sharp)$$

As an application, we will deduce Bochner's vanishing theorem: if $\operatorname{Ric} > 0$ on a closed, oriented manifold $\mathcal{M}$, then its first Betti number vanishes, $b_1(\mathcal{M}) = 0$.

Prerequisites Basic multi-variable calculus and definitions of smooth manifolds. No previous knowledge of Riemannian curvature tensors is required; all geometric notions will be introduced with sketches!
